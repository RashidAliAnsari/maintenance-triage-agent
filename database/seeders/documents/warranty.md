# Appliance Warranty Schedule

Northgate Property Management LLC
Manufacturer Warranty Terms — Appliances Supplied With Units
Current as at January 2026

---

## Purpose

This schedule records the warranty terms applying to appliances supplied by the
Landlord. Where a reported defect concerns an appliance still within its
warranty period, the repair is handled as a manufacturer warranty claim and
neither the Landlord nor the Tenant bears the repair cost.

---

## Standard Warranty Periods by Appliance Type

**Refrigerators and freezers.** Twenty-four months from date of installation on
parts and labour. The sealed refrigeration system, including compressor,
condenser, and evaporator, carries an extended sixty-month warranty on parts.
Door seals, shelving, and interior fittings are excluded after twelve months.

**Ovens, cookers, and hobs.** Twenty-four months from date of installation on
parts and labour. Heating elements and thermostats are covered in full. Glass
panels, door hinges, and control knobs are excluded where damage is
consistent with impact or misuse.

**Dishwashers.** Twenty-four months from date of installation. Pumps, motors,
and control boards are covered. Blockages arising from food debris, and damage
from using incorrect detergent, are excluded and treated as misuse.

**Washing machines and dryers.** Twenty-four months from date of installation.
Drums, motors, bearings, and control boards are covered. Damage from overloading
or from washing items containing foreign objects is excluded.

**Microwave ovens.** Twelve months from date of installation. The magnetron
carries an extended thirty-six month warranty on parts only.

**Extractor hoods and ventilation units.** Twelve months from date of
installation. Filters are consumable and excluded in all cases.

**Water heaters.** Sixty months on the tank, twenty-four months on the heating
element and thermostat. Damage from scale accumulation where the Tenant has
failed to report reduced performance may be excluded.

---

## Exclusions Applying to All Appliances

A warranty claim will not be accepted where:

- The defect arises from misuse, neglect, or accidental damage
- The appliance has been repaired or modified by an unauthorised person
- Consumable parts have failed through normal wear, including filters, bulbs,
  seals after twelve months, and detachable fittings
- The appliance has been operated outside its stated specification, including
  overloading and use with incorrect consumables
- The defect arises from a supply fault — water pressure, electrical supply, or
  drainage — rather than from the appliance itself

Where a claim is refused on grounds of Tenant misuse, the repair becomes the
Tenant's responsibility under Clause 13.1 of the lease.

Where a claim is refused because the warranty period has expired, the repair
becomes the Landlord's responsibility under Clause 12.5 of the lease.

---

## Determining Warranty Status

**Step 1.** Identify the appliance type and the unit in which it is installed.

**Step 2.** Retrieve the installation date from the unit inventory schedule.

**Step 3.** Compare the elapsed period against the standard warranty period for
that appliance type, noting any extended component coverage.

**Step 4.** Consider whether the reported symptoms fall within an exclusion. A
dishwasher that will not drain following reported disposal of food waste falls
under misuse; a dishwasher whose pump has failed with no contributing factor
falls under warranty.

**Step 5.** Where installation date cannot be established, or where the reported
symptoms are consistent with both a covered fault and an excluded cause,
responsibility is unclear and the matter should be referred for human review.

---

## Claim Procedure

Warranty claims are made directly to the manufacturer's service line, not
through the contractor panel. The manufacturer appoints its own engineer.
Response times are set by the manufacturer and are typically five to ten
business days, which may be unsuitable where the appliance failure affects
habitability. Where a warranty claim would leave the unit without hot water,
heating, or cooking facilities for more than seventy-two hours, the Property
Manager may instruct a contractor and pursue reimbursement separately.
