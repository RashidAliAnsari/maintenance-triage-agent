# Maintenance Standard Operating Procedure

Northgate Property Management LLC
Handbook for Property Managers — Request Handling
Revision 7, January 2026

---

## 1. Urgency Classification

Every reported defect is assigned one of three urgency levels. Classification
drives the response window and determines whether out of hours rates apply.

### 1.1 Emergency — attend within four hours

A defect presenting an immediate risk to the safety of occupants, to the
security of the premises, or of substantial and continuing damage to the
building. Emergencies are attended at any hour.

Examples:

- Burst pipe, or any uncontrolled water escape
- Total loss of water supply
- Exposed live wiring, burning smell from electrical fittings, or scorch marks
- Total loss of electrical supply to the unit
- Total loss of heating between November and March
- Gas odour, suspected leak, or carbon monoxide alarm activation
- Roof failure allowing water ingress into the living space
- Failure of the only entrance door or its lock, leaving the unit insecure
- Blocked or overflowing soil stack affecting the only toilet
- Any structural movement, ceiling collapse, or falling masonry

### 1.2 Urgent — attend within twenty-four hours

A defect materially affecting the use of the premises but not presenting an
immediate risk.

Examples:

- Loss of hot water where cold supply is unaffected
- Single circuit failure affecting kitchen or bathroom
- Heating failure between April and October
- Blocked toilet where a second toilet is available
- Refrigerator or freezer failure
- Cooker or oven failure
- Water ingress not affecting living space, including a leaking roof over an
  outbuilding or a leak contained within a cupboard
- Failure of a secondary entrance door or window lock

### 1.3 Routine — attend within five business days

A defect causing inconvenience but not materially affecting the use of the
premises.

Examples:

- Dripping tap
- Slow-draining sink or bath
- Individual socket or light fitting not working
- Dishwasher, washing machine, or dryer failure
- Extractor fan failure
- Door sticking or failing to latch cleanly
- Cosmetic damage, including cracked tiles and damaged plaster
- Minor mould in a bathroom or on a window reveal

## 2. Category Assignment

Requests are categorised by the system affected, not by the room in which the
defect appears.

**Plumbing** — supply pipes, waste pipes, drains, taps, toilets, showers, water
heaters.

**Electrical** — fixed wiring, sockets, switches, consumer units, permanently
installed lighting.

**HVAC** — heating systems, air conditioning, thermostats, ventilation
ductwork, extractor systems.

**Appliance** — free-standing and integrated appliances supplied with the unit,
including refrigerators, ovens, dishwashers, washing machines, dryers, and
microwaves.

**Structural** — roof, walls, floors, ceilings, windows, doors, stairs,
foundations, and any damp or mould arising from the building fabric.

**Other** — anything not falling into the above, including pest infestations,
smoke alarms, garage doors, external grounds, and lock or key issues.

### 2.1 Boundary cases

Some defects could reasonably fall into two categories. Apply these
conventions:

- A leaking dishwasher or washing machine is **appliance**, unless the leak is
  traced to the supply or waste connection, in which case it is **plumbing**
- No hot water is **plumbing** where the water heater is a plumbed system, and
  **appliance** where a standalone electric heater is at fault
- A leaking radiator is **HVAC**
- Damp arising from a plumbing leak is **plumbing**; damp arising from the
  building fabric is **structural**
- An extractor fan is **HVAC** where ducted, **electrical** where the failure is
  in the supply

## 3. Escalation to a Human Property Manager

The following conditions require the request to be escalated rather than
assigned automatically. Escalation is not a failure state; it is the correct
outcome where automated handling would carry unacceptable risk.

**3.1 Estimated cost exceeds the owner approval threshold.** Any repair
estimated above five hundred dollars requires the Owner's written approval
before instruction. See the Management Agreement, Schedule C, Section 2.2.

**3.2 Responsibility cannot be determined.** Where the available policy
documents do not establish whether the Landlord, the Tenant, or a manufacturer
warranty bears the cost, the matter is referred for human determination. See
Lease Clause 16.

**3.3 The request is classified as an emergency.** Emergencies carry liability
exposure and are reviewed by a human before any contractor is instructed,
without exception, and regardless of estimated cost.

**3.4 Classification confidence is low.** Where the description is too vague,
too brief, or internally inconsistent to categorise reliably, the request is
escalated rather than guessed.

**3.5 No contractor is available within the response window.** Where the panel
cannot attend within the applicable window, the Property Manager seeks an
alternative or notifies the Owner.

**3.6 No relevant policy is found.** Where a search of the policy documents
returns nothing bearing on the request, responsibility is treated as unclear.

**3.7 The trade falls outside the contractor panel.** Roofing requiring access
equipment, gas work, pest control, asbestos, and structural assessment are
quoted job by job and are not assigned automatically.

**3.8 A duplicate request is suspected.** Where an open request already exists
for the same unit and a similar defect, the new request is flagged for review
rather than generating a second work order.

## 4. Cost Estimation

Estimated cost is calculated as the expected job duration multiplied by the
hourly rate of the selected contractor, plus any applicable emergency callout
charge. Refer to the Contractor Rate Sheet for both figures.

Where a job could reasonably take a range of hours, the upper figure is used
for the purposes of the approval threshold. A repair estimated at two to four
hours at ninety-five dollars per hour is treated as three hundred and eighty
dollars, not one hundred and ninety.

Material costs are not included in the automated estimate and are billed
separately. Where materials are expected to be significant, the request should
be escalated for a proper quotation.

---

## 5. Record Keeping

Every request must carry a complete record of the classification applied, the
policy provisions relied upon in determining responsibility, the contractor
selected and the basis for selection, and the final outcome. Where a request is
escalated, the reason must be recorded.

This record supports the monthly report to the Owner and is the primary
evidence in any dispute over cost allocation.
